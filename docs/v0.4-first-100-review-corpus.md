# KhasiLex v0.4 — First 100-Entry Khasi Review Corpus

Batch: `v0.4-pilot-001`

This is the first real source-attested 100-entry Khasi review corpus in KhasiLex.

## What has been reviewed already

The automated/editorial-preparation pass has checked and recorded:

- Khasi language code `kha`;
- Unicode NFC normalization;
- source registration and licence compatibility;
- attestation in English Wiktionary Khasi part-of-speech categories;
- source part-of-speech evidence;
- whether the entry is also listed in Wiktionary's Khasi IPA category;
- whether the entry is also listed in Wiktionary's Khasi usage-example category;
- compound and multiword category evidence where applicable;
- whether the headword already exists in the KhasiLex master lexicon;
- known POS-overlap cases that require sense separation.

This is **source/evidence review**, not final Khasi linguistic approval.

## Important review cases

### Existing KhasiLex seed entries

Five pilot headwords already exist in the master lexicon:

- `um`
- `ïing`
- `nga`
- `ngi`
- `u`

They must be upgraded in place after review rather than added as duplicate master entries.

### POS/sense overlap

The batch intentionally preserves source-attested ambiguity:

- `dei` — attested as both verb and adjective;
- `phira` — attested as both noun and verb.

These require separate sense/POS decisions instead of collapsing them into a single gloss.

### Spelling review

`pyniap` is retained exactly as attested in the source category, while `pynïap` is also independently attested in Wiktionary. A Khasi reviewer must decide whether these are variants, distinct forms, or a source inconsistency. KhasiLex must not silently replace one with the other.

### Multiword / compound evidence

The pilot includes source-attested compound and multiword cases, including forms such as `at-khu` and `sing-mana`. Their internal morphology and canonical representation still require linguistic review.

## Human review worksheet

Use:

`data/review/batches/v0.4-pilot-001-human-review.csv`

For every candidate, the reviewer should decide or supply:

1. canonical spelling;
2. accepted part of speech;
3. Khasi definition;
4. English definition/gloss;
5. Khasi example sentence;
6. English example translation if appropriate;
7. dialect/register information;
8. variant relationships;
9. sense notes;
10. reviewer name and date;
11. decision: `accept`, `revise`, `reject`, or `defer`.

Only after a competent Khasi reviewer completes these fields may an entry progress from `pending` to `reviewed`.

`verified` requires the stronger production gate in `docs/v0.4-editorial-policy.md`.

## Automated audit

Run:

```bash
python scripts/audit_review_batch.py
```

The audit checks:

- exactly 100 entries;
- expected POS distribution;
- unique candidate IDs;
- NFC normalization;
- `kha` language code;
- approved registered source;
- source licence consistency;
- category/POS consistency;
- correct `existing_master` flags;
- mandatory human-review flag;
- pending-only verification state.

The audit is also invoked from `scripts/build_all.py`, so CI will reject structural or provenance regressions.

## Next review sequence

Review priority is encoded in the batch:

- **Priority 1** — existing master entries or entries with source usage-example evidence;
- **Priority 2** — entries with IPA evidence or other useful source metadata;
- **Priority 3** — source-attested POS/headword candidates needing fuller evidence.

The recommended workflow is to finish Priority 1 first, then Priority 2, then Priority 3.

## Editorial principle

A word being present in Wiktionary or a corpus proves only that it is an attested candidate for review. It does not, by itself, prove that the spelling, definition, grammatical analysis, register, dialect, or translation is authoritative Khasi.
