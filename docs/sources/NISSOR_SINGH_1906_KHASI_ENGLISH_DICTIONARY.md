## Batch 003

All 20 candidate headwords are transcribed on printed pages **1–3** (Wikisource export PDF pages approximately 7–10, with printed page 2 spanning an export page break). Historical meanings and POS originate from this **same 1906 source**; they are not independent corroboration. Author's preface notes a preference for the Cherra/Sohra dialect. The explanatory notes describe morphological affixes and historical spellings.

Independent learner's-dictionary evidence from user-supplied Bharatavani screenshots corroborates **Ahor** ('marsh') and **A-iu** (as `ai-u`, 'what'). Eighteen candidate headwords were not listed in that selective learner's dictionary. These omissions are **not** evidence of invalidity or obsolescence. The 1920 English–Khasi reverse lookup, grammar corroboration, and modern sense attestations remain outstanding. **No lexical promotions or merge without explicit owner acceptance.**

## Source preservation policy

Keep provenance, printed page numbers, exact historical forms, edition, and source-specific sense separate from modern standardized forms. **Editorial rule:** The 1920 reverse dictionary is associated with Nissor Singh and editors also associated with the 1906 work. An exact reverse match, if directly inspected, is valuable **cross-edition corroboration**, but should **not automatically be called an independent authorial witness**. Record both the matching English lemma and the exact Khasi equivalent with the original 1920 printed page. A catalog entry or PDF existence alone does not establish any lexical match.
